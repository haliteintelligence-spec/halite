Halite Connect

What ships when

The pilot is the smallest thing a brand will pay for: sync a catalog, let a shopper connect, rank the catalog against them, report what converted. Access has no timer yet and a brand never learns whose bottles are on a shelf — only what they are made of. Everything after it deepens the same four moves: finer permission, more surfaces, then an ownership model the brand rents rather than owns.

Merchant side

Categories set at brand signup — they decide what Connect may request
Catalog sync — Shopify, CSV/XLSX, API
Attribute enrichment per category (notes, intensity, ingredients)
Product-match API returning scores and reasons
Conversion events back from the store
Performance and audience-insight dashboard

Consumer side

Connect widget on product, quiz and collection pages
Hallie account connection, no new signup
Category consent screen — whole set, one decision
Cross-brand products shared as notes and ingredients only — never another brand's name; a brand sees its own products in full
Save-to-wishlist from the brand's storefront, written back to the shopper's Hallie collection
Access runs until the shopper disconnects — no timer yet

The dashboard only has to answer five things: who connected, what they want, what we recommended, what converted, and what the catalog is missing.

Next

Consumer control

Turn the one-decision consent into a real permissions product.

Field-level consent — the shopper unticks individual items before allowing
Time-limited access — session, 30 days or a year, with automatic expiry and renewal
Shopper can opt in to naming specific products from another brand
Purpose separation — personalization and marketing are different grants
Consent ledger, access log and deletion queue in the brand dashboard
Hallie permission centre: connected brands, extend, disconnect
Three access tiers a brand can buy: ranked products only, derived summary, or approved fields
Campaign audiences out to Klaviyo and Braze, on marketing consent only

Built on the pilot's grant record — the pilot writes one row per grant, this phase gives that row a shape.

Underneath, over time

Ownership moves

The B2B product stays familiar SaaS while the identity model beneath it decentralizes.

Portable decentralized identifier behind the Halite ID
Signed purchase and outcome credentials — a shopper can prove they own a product
Consumer-held encrypted storage
Revocable access keys, enforced cryptographically rather than by our server
Interoperable identity standards, so a profile outlives Halite
Rewards for verified contributions, paid from what brands pay for access

The web3 story is not the interface. It is that brands rent permissioned intelligence instead of capturing and permanently owning a consumer.
